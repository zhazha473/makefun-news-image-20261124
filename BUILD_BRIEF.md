## 2. How we differ (explicit — same principles, different work)

- **Format: "THE WORLD CLOCK"** — NOT a newspaper front page (the winners' format),
  NOT atlas/timeline (19 Nov), NOT a wire board (20 Nov), NOT city cards (21 Nov),
  NOT timestamped wire dispatches (22 Nov), NOT a departures board (23 Nov). The day
  is told as one turn of a 24-hour clock dial: a large dial is the composition's spine,
  each story pinned at the UTC hour it broke (small clock-face hour marker per story),
  the hero story at the dial's centre. Masthead, story rows with city + headline +
  UTC time + source tag, DATE CHECK strip, STILL TO COME footer. Same principles
  (date unmissable, counted stories, breadth, source tags), different work.
- **Hero: the World Chess Championship, Game 1** — Gukesh vs Sindarov in Geneva,
  15:00 CET, the youngest title match in history, $2.5m prize. Draw the two kings /
  a chessboard motif at the dial's centre — not a generic globe, not a UN chamber.
- **Time framing: "FIRST EDITION — as reported by 00:xx UTC"** (real hour). The day is
  minutes old in UTC; list what has actually happened or been first reported by then,
  plus a "STILL TO COME TODAY" footer (Japan PMI 00:30 UTC, Germany Ifo 09:00 GMT,
  US ADP + consumer confidence + Case-Shiller later Tuesday) — never present scheduled
  events as if they already happened, and never invent 35 stories that do not exist yet.
- **A DATE CHECK strip on the image** naming the big 22–23 Nov stories deliberately
  left out — proof of verification, not decoration (see §4).
- **Every story source-tagged on the image** (Reuters / FIDE / KCNA / etc.).
- **Title wording is our own:** counts stories + names the world-clock format and the
  hero stories; no "front page", no "35 stories", no "7 regions", no colour "edition".
